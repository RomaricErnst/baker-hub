# Bakerhub engine audit

Read-only audit of main commit 7a118048886dadb572c426d0643db1dedd4f5b80. Production TypeScript `app/utils.ts` and `app/data.ts` were transpiled into scratch JavaScript outside the repository and their exported functions executed. No implementation edits. Findings below distinguish reproduced engine results from UI integration requiring follow-up. Scientific validation of coefficients is a separate workstream.

## Verified findings

### P1 — Small poolishes can contain zero yeast (high confidence)
`app/data.ts:992–998` rounds IDY seed to 0.1 g **before** yeast conversion; quantities below 0.05 g become zero. `app/components/RecipeOutput.tsx:732` hides the yeast ingredient when zero; final-dough yeast is also suppressed whenever a preferment exists (`:473`). Reproduced through `calculateRecipe`: Neapolitan, pizza oven, one 250 g ball, 24°C, 20% RT poolish, 12 h poolish window, 24 h cold + 4 h RT dough gives 154 g flour and **0 g poolish yeast**. At 32°C, even a 6 h poolish gives zero. For the controlled schedule, dates were Sep 11 00:00 UTC bulk to Sep 12 04:00 UTC bake. A UI-generated plan still needs replay, but the recipe function itself demonstrably produces an unleavened recipe. Preserve precision until display; dilution instructions should cover preferments too.

### P1 — Target dough mass excludes oil and sugar (high confidence)
`app/utils.ts:1207–1213` divides desired weight by flour + hydration + salt, then adds oil and sugar afterward. It also omits yeast mass. Executed 1,000 g custom recipes at 32°C, standard bread oven (pan: standard home oven), 24 h cold + 4 h RT: pan returns flour 580 + water 406 + salt 15 + oil 23 + sugar 2.9 + yeast .408 = **1,027.308 g**; pain viennois returns 626 + 363 + 11 + 13 + 31.3 + .529 = **1,044.829 g**. Yeast omission alone is small; enrichment omission materially changes piece weights and shopping quantities. Include all ingredient fractions in the mass balance.

### P1 — Named enriched breads do not have their defining ingredients (high confidence)
`app/data.ts:200–219` calls brioche buttery but represents only flour, water, salt, yeast, sugar; zero oil and no egg or butter fields. `app/utils.ts:1386–1392` can only return those ingredient categories. Pain viennois is described as milk-enriched (`app/data.ts:241` onward) but likewise has no milk. This is a product/recipe correctness gap, not a minor stylistic preference. The app needs explicit enrichment quantities and water accounting, or narrower names and claims. The thermal model also cannot account for the temperature of missing butter/eggs/milk.

### P1 — Impossible water targets are silently clipped (high confidence)
`app/utils.ts:615–620` computes ideal water temperature and attainable dough temperature, but `calculateRecipe` (`:1374–1383`) retains only water temperature clipped to 2–40°C. Reproduced Neapolitan 1,000 g, pizza oven, 38°C kitchen/flour, spiral mixer: target FDT 23°C, ideal water **−2.003°C**, supplied water **2°C**, predicted dough **25.271°C**. The recipe has no attainable-FDT output. `RecipeOutput.tsx:507` produces ice guidance to obtain the clipped water target, rather than correcting the remaining dough heat deficit. Display the residual temperature error and a feasible intervention (chill flour/preferment, adjust mixing, or model ice directly). This is especially relevant to the user's hot-climate goal.

### P2 — More room-temperature time can increase recommended yeast (high confidence)
`app/utils.ts:161–178` ignores RT exposure entirely through 4 h whenever a cold phase exists, then abruptly switches to a weighted formula. Executed at 35°C, 24 h cold, fridge 6°C: IDY recommendation is .0704% at RT 3.9 and 4 h, then **.0779% at 4.01 and 4.1 h** (+10.7%). Same flavor priority in all cases. A threshold introducing extra yeast with extra warm exposure is a consistency defect independent of whether the underlying empirical model is biologically validated. Replace the discontinuous blend only after agreeing on a calibrated multi-stage model.

### P2 — Dilution instructions become stale after yeast correction (high confidence)
`app/utils.ts:283–288` builds dilution instructions before flour tolerance and sugar changes (`:1252–1300`). Reproduced brioche described above: final dose .538 g, but dilution solution is 44.8 g, corresponding to approximately .448 g under the app's own 1:100 convention — the pre-sugar dose. `RecipeOutput.tsx:1224` renders the stale dilution object. Recompute all dependent guidance from the final converted dose. Separately, 1 g yeast + 100 g water is 101 g solution, so the current solution calculation is about 1% low even without modifiers (minor).

### P2 — Existing yeast “tests” do not exercise the production engine (high confidence)
`app/utils/yeastEngine.test.ts:18–91` reimplements an alternate engine locally: 2% ceiling vs production 1.5%, different tropical bounds, and additive/bisection mixed fermentation vs production weighted blend. There is no import of the production implementation. Test helpers only log FAIL without throwing. `package.json` has no test script. These checks cannot detect the demonstrated production regression. Add exported pure engine contracts and real assertions covering mass conservation, conversion, positive seed, temperature bounds, and monotonicity.

## Current logic map and important limitations

- Hydration: style baseline + oven delta (half for enriched/high-oil), minus 2 points at ≥28°C OR very humid, plus 2 at ≤18°C, flour-blend delta clamped −5/+8; per-style floors. Exact custom hydration bypasses adjustments. The climate correction is a heuristic, not a measured flour-moisture model.
- Yeast: empirical RT power law 9.5/(h^1.65 × 2.5^((T−25)/10)); extra tropical division 1.15 at ≥30 and 1.25 at ≥33. Cold law 7.5/h^1.313 with Q10=2 relative to 4°C. Percent floor .05; auto flavor ×.7 or speed ×1.8; max 1.5%. Thus automatic schedule-duration thresholds independently change dose. Salt and dough hydration do not enter this calculation; sugar above 2% gets a flat +20%.
- Schedule: style fixed cold/RT defaults, quarter-hour rounding, availability windows, single/two-phase refrigeration. `buildSchedule` has no fridge-temperature argument. It uses warm/cool bands for RT minima, and style preferences for cold duration. Actual gradual cooling and warming of a batch are not simulated by the fermentation engine. Recipe size affects ingredient amounts, not main dough cooling kinetics.
- Preferments: poolish 100% hydration and biga fixed hydration/fraction with overrides. Biga is forcibly cold in `computePrefermentRecipe` even if its supplied boolean is false (`data.ts:921`). Seed is max of peak-ripening requirement and an exponential population-growth estimate; the latter is anchored to two stated calibration points, not a published validation dataset. This is a major science-review target. Returning `cold` and schedule text uses static type data (`data.ts:1002–1012`), not effective cold selection; inspect consumers before calling this a user-visible defect.
- Sourdough dose: temperature bands 10–15%, 15–20%, or 20–25% starter by total flour; feed-to-mix ratio and flour strength adjust this. It assumes **100% starter hydration**, subtracting half starter mass from flour and water (`RecipeOutput.tsx:452–456`). Dough schedule length itself is not an argument of `sourdoughGuidance`; the complex starter scheduler handles feed timing and ratio separately. Starter maturity, rye, location, last feeding, repeated refreshes, blockers and fridge holds are represented in SchedulePicker. Non-100% starter hydration is not a supported recipe input in this path.
- DDT is now an enthalpy balance, with flour/water/preferment heat capacities and mixer heat. Preferment warmup uses an approximate mass/hydration thermal model. Mixing friction uses fixed mixer time rather than each style's actual mixing duration (explicitly documented in source). Levain is assumed to reach kitchen temperature, which is stronger than merely taking it out of the fridge.
- Style coverage includes six pizza styles and nine breads, but named availability should not be mistaken for validated style-specific science. Temperature ranges up to 38°C exceed the legacy sourdough handoff's stated 16/24/32°C matrix.

## Architecture and evidence caveats

CLAUDE.md and SOURDOUGH_PEAK_MODEL_HANDOFF.md are stale: the former still describes the old three-factor DDT and hard .5 g floor; the latter says peak2A is un-unified, but current `SchedulePicker.tsx:198`, `:4403`, and `:5452` call the shared `computeStarterPeakMs` helper. **Do not report the old peak2A mismatch as a confirmed present bug.** A live matrix is still required to establish card/bell/scoring parity for all candidate families; static inspection cannot prove that invariant across interactive state.

Residual .5 g floors remain in flour tolerance and preferment-reduction branches (`utils.ts:1255,1275–1276`). Flour tolerance changes IDY grams and converted grams differently; those branches merit a dedicated recipe/UI trace. Preferment final yeast is hidden, so do not equate every stored yeast-object error with a displayed extra yeast dose.

## Additional cross-check with coordinator's live observation

**P1/P2 — Wall-clock proof and yeast exposure disagree (high confidence source + live observation).** The coordinator observed Simple Neapolitan at 32°C with four 260 g balls, home oven stone, hand kneading, Sep 13 18:00 bake: protocol phase summary says 1 h 30 final proof, detailed row says Sep 13 15:30 → 18:00, 2 h 30. `Timeline.tsx:366–367` correctly calculates detailed wall-clock duration; `:436` uses warmup + schedule.finalProofHours. In `utils.ts:882–885`, finalProofHours is capped by maxFinalH even if the actual elapsed proof is longer; `:909` then includes the capped duration in totalRTHours. This is therefore potentially a dosing error as well as a display discrepancy. Store actual durations separately from recommended bounds; flag exceeded bounds instead of shortening accounting time.

**P2 — Ice recipe assumes ambient water but UI says cold water (high confidence).** Coordinator saw target 14°C, 64 g ice + 331 g 'cold water' in a 32°C kitchen. `RecipeOutput.tsx:212` computes with ambientTemp supplied as kitchenTemp (`:507`). The equation is appropriate for water at 32°C: (331×32 −64×79.8)/395 ≈13.88°C. If the baker follows 'cold water' using 6°C refrigerated water, heat is insufficient to melt all ice: about 24.9 g melts, leaving about 39.1 g ice, and the mixture is near 0°C. The strain-before-using instruction can then remove recipe water as remaining ice. Ask for/source actual starting water temperature or make the ambient-water assumption explicit.
